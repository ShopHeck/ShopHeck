Fight Camp · Frontend design review · August 2026

Three ways out
of the card farm.

The app underneath is strong: a periodized camp generator, a least-squares weight-cut projection, a readiness engine that learns from fight results. The interface presents all of it as the same rounded rectangle, forty times over. These are three directions that fix that, each committing to a different idea about what this product actually is.

Baseline · shipping build

What ships today

Every element is a 12px-radius card on #1a1a1a with a #2a2a2a border. Nothing is louder than anything else, so the eye has nowhere to land.

The problem, stated plainly

Seven accent hues compete at equal volume on one screen. Orange on near-black is the first thing anyone reaches for when the brief says “combat sports”, which means the app looks like every rival on the shelf.

Where it breaks down

  • One elevation. Card, stat card, tool tile and empty state share a border, radius and fill.
  • Colour carries no meaning. Purple marks AI, teal marks trackers, yellow marks strength. It is decoration wearing a uniform.
  • #6b7280 on #1a1a1a is 4.1:1. Below AA for the sizes it runs at, and it is the default for secondary copy everywhere.
  • Six bottom tabs against Apple's five, and ten of the twenty views are not reachable from any of them.

Direction 01

Tale of the Tape

The app as the official record of your camp. Manila stock, ink, hairline rules, and one red stamp held back for the things that actually matter.

Where this comes from

Not from fitness apps. From the paperwork of the sport: sanctioning-body weigh-in cards, Ring Magazine's tale of the tape, the record book behind the desk at the commission. Figures set in columns, names set in type, nothing decorated.

Rules of the direction

  • No cards. Rules and ledger rows carry every grouping. Nothing floats on a fill.
  • Light ground. Legible on a gym floor under fluorescents, where near-black at low brightness is not.
  • Live screens invert. The timer flips to ink so it reads from across the mat. State is the only thing that changes theme.
  • Red is a stamp, not a brand colour. It marks the cut and the countdown. Nothing else gets it.

What this direction fixes

Hierarchy without elevation

Rules and ledger rows replace forty identical cards. Weight given by type size and position, not by another border.

removes .card, .stat-card

Readable in a bright gym

Ink on manila holds up under overhead fluorescents at half brightness, where #0a0a0a turns into a mirror.

light ground, 13:1 body contrast

Figures that compare

Tabular numerals throughout, so needed rate sits directly above actual rate and the difference is visible without arithmetic.

font-variant-numeric

An identity nobody else has

Every competitor is orange or red on black. A record-book aesthetic is unclaimed, and it flatters an app whose real asset is its data.

category-reflex avoided

Trade-off

The largest build of the three. A light ground means auditing every one of the 30 components for hardcoded dark values, and the two-theme system (document light, gym-floor ink) has to be real rather than a filter. Budget the most time here.

Direction 02

Instrument

A readout, not a feed. Tinted graphite, brass for one accent, and colour spent only where it means something clinical.

Where this comes from

The app already runs a least-squares projection on the cut and scores readiness from load, sleep and HRV. That is laboratory work wearing a consumer costume. This direction takes the costume off: gauge faces, tick marks, monospaced figures, and a chassis quiet enough to read at 9pm in bed at low brightness.

Rules of the direction

  • Colour equals state. Green, amber and rust mean on track, watch, intervene. Nothing else is ever coloured.
  • Every figure is monospaced, so a column of weights reads as a column and drift is visible without a chart.
  • Hairlines, not boxes. One panel fill for the whole screen; rows divide with 1px at 12% opacity.
  • Gauges state their value in text. The readiness score stops being invisible to screen readers.

Direction 03

Corner

Sixty seconds on the stool. One screen answers one question, the surface itself carries the state, and nothing needs a second look.

Where this comes from

Arm's length, sweat on the glass, heart rate at 170, three seconds of attention before the next round. Everything that survives that test stays on the screen; everything that does not moves behind More. This is the only direction that changes the information architecture rather than the paint.

Palette — committed, state-driven

Rules of the direction

  • The surface is the state. Work is blue, rest is green, the last ten seconds are amber. No full-screen flash overlay.
  • One primary action per screen, at 58px minimum, always in the bottom third where a thumb reaches.
  • Four tabs and a centre action, down from six. Everything else lives behind a real More screen with search.
  • Nothing under 44pt is tappable, anywhere, including delete controls that are currently hover-only.

Six tabs become four

Targets that survive wraps

Primary controls at 58 to 74px, secondary at 44px minimum, all in the lower third. Nothing important sits under a thumb's reach or above it.

Trade-off

This is the direction that moves features, not just pixels, so it carries product risk as well as design risk: anything demoted to More gets used less, and if the wrong things go behind it, engagement drops before it recovers. It is also the loudest of the three. A drenched blue timer is a strong opinion, and strong opinions get one-star reviews as well as five-star ones.

Recommendation

The navigation is the expensive problem. The palette is the cheap one.

Ten of the twenty views cannot be reached from the tab bar, and Header is never passed the showBack prop it already supports, so the Home tab is the only way out of any of them. The empty-state gap is largely closed already: NoCampState now covers the planner, log and weight views and the dashboard has its own mode chooser, leaving only fight-log and fight-breakdown rendering nothing when their preconditions fail. Routing is the part no repaint fixes. Corner is the only direction that touches it, which makes its four-tab structure worth taking regardless of which palette wins.

On the visual system, the honest ranking depends on what this release is for. If it is a quality pass, Instrument costs the least and fixes the most per hour: it keeps the dark ground, repairs contrast at the token level, collapses seven accents into one plus a semantic ramp, and pulls load ratio and HRV up from three screens deep. If it is a repositioning, Tale of the Tape is the only one that will be remembered, because every competitor on the shelf is already orange or red on black.

Pick a direction and I will build it: tokens first, then the shared chrome, then screen by screen behind a flag so nothing ships half-converted.

Least risk · fastest
Instrument

Retunes what exists. No layout rewrites, no light-mode audit. Contrast, accent discipline and data density improve on every screen at once.

Most distinctive
Tale of the Tape

Buys an identity nobody in the category has. Costs a full light-theme audit across 30 components and a second theme for live screens.

Biggest usability gain
Corner

The only one that fixes navigation, tap targets and the ten-section dashboard. Carries product risk: anything demoted to More gets used less.

What I would do
Corner's structure, Instrument's tokens

Take the four-tab architecture and the empty states from 03, dress them in the palette and figure treatment from 02. Revisit 01 when identity, not usability, is the priority.
